# Developer ROI

Research and proposed value model, 12 October 2026. **Spend less time explaining, triaging, and rebuilding interfaces, while helping people work independently.** Report recovered hours before monetary value. All examples are hypothetical; the product has no measured return.

## Benefit ledger

| Benefit | Measure | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fewer confusion-driven reports | Relevant contacts per 1,000 workflow attempts; handling/escalation effort | Support and engineering capacity |
| Faster diagnosis | Active classification/resolution minutes, including reopenings | Maintainer capacity |
| Less rework | UI-related rework across comparable releases | Observed reduction or explicitly forecast avoided work |
| Better developer tools | Correct independent completion, total effort, recovery, context switches | Developer-user capacity |
| Easier onboarding | Learner and mentor effort to a defined capability | Two roles' capacity, counted separately |
| More affordable research | Staff effort and external cost per useful, sufficiently valid study | Incremental benefit over current research |
| Better product outcomes | Deployment activation, conversion, or continued use | Contribution margin when attribution/accounting support it |
| Greater access | Completion and help by relevant access condition | Participant value; critical access requirements remain explicit |

The Lean UX principles of UX debt (`lux-principle-ux-debt`) and outcomes over output (`lux-principle-outcomes-over-output`) motivate tracking unresolved experience costs. Study counts, generated findings, and closed issues do not establish return.

### Confusion-driven “bug” reports

“Save is broken” may describe a successful operation with no understandable confirmation. Review possible causes: implementation defect, feedback, discoverability, documentation/setup, workflow mismatch, access barrier, or unresolved. Multiple causes can coexist; “cannot reproduce” does not determine them. Agents propose evidence-linked classifications. Relabeling/closing reports earns no ROI credit.

Normalize contacts by exposure; adoption can increase raw volume despite improvement. Include repeat contacts/escalations and preserve completion, abandonment, and access to support as guardrails. Blocking complaints or losing users must not appear beneficial.

Avoid counting prevention and faster handling twice. At equivalent exposure, let `Q0/Q1` be before/after contact counts and `h0/h1` handling hours:

```text
Recovered hours = Q0*h0 - Q1*h1
                = (Q0-Q1)*h0 + Q1*(h0-h1)
```

Faster handling applies only to remaining contacts.

### Developer and agent tools

Evaluate realistic configuration, authentication, API discovery, failed-build diagnosis, permissions, and recovery. Recruit relevant newcomers and experienced users separately. The initial web runner covers portals, documentation, dashboards, and browser environments; native IDEs/terminals require additional adapters.

Count unsuccessful effort and assistance. First-call speed can conceal later integration failure. Google's [API Usability at Scale](https://ppig.org/files/2016-PPIG-27th-Macvean.pdf) examines authentication, misconceptions, resources, and context switches; it supplies a precedent, not dollar returns. [DevEx](https://www.michaelagreiler.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/DevEx-WhatDrivesProductivity.pdf) suggests feedback loops, cognitive load, and flow; [SPACE](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-space-of-developer-productivity-theres-more-to-it-than-you-think/) calls for multiple productivity dimensions. Neither provides a financial multiplier.

Illustration: `40 developers × 20 uses/month × 1.5 minutes saved / 60 = 20 hours/month`, valued at an assumed `$100/hour = $2,000/month` of capacity. Measure active effort, blocking waits, and interruptions: overlapping waits and fragmented seconds do not automatically become usable development time. Onboarding value is `new users × (learner hours saved × learner rate + mentor hours saved × mentor rate)`; shorter calendar ramp-up is different, and overlapping recurring-task minutes count once.

For agent DX, hold task mix and required correctness fixed:

```text
Cost per verified correct task = all operational attempt costs
                                / verified correct completions
```

Include failures/retries, inference, tools, infrastructure, and relevant human correction. Report metered expenditure separately from valued labor; zero successes makes the ratio undefined. Research grading belongs in study investment, not silently in production execution. Fewer calls/takeovers can conceal omitted work or approvals; faster agent wall time is not automatically human capacity.

Illustration: among 1,000 attempts, reducing unnecessary takeovers from 8% to 5% at six minutes recovers three hours (`$300` at the assumed rate). Reducing all-attempt model/tool cost from `$0.08` to `$0.06` saves `$20` cash. Both precede study, fix, verification, and maintenance costs. A cheaper model meeting the same criterion is a separate substitution experiment; additional correct work needs its own business valuation.

## Accounting contract

Every input records beneficiary, comparison, horizon, adoption/exposure, evidence, and uncertainty. Distinguish **capacity**, **cash actually avoided**, and **incremental contribution margin**. Salaried hours recovered are not payroll reductions; customers' saved time is not automatically vendor revenue. Retain satisfaction/confidence/access without arbitrary dollar conversion.

| Channel | Calculation |
| --- | --- |
| Prevented contacts | `exposures × attributable contact-rate reduction × handling hours/contact` |
| Faster triage | `remaining contacts × attributable handling-hour reduction` |
| Routine tasks | `attempts × attributable total-effort reduction/attempt` |
| Onboarding | `new users × role-specific effort reductions`, valued at each role's rate |
| Rework | `comparable baseline hours − intervention hours`; future avoidance remains forecast |
| Research operations | `baseline research cost − library research cost` for comparable scope/quality; already net |
| Commercial outcomes | `opportunities × attributable outcome-rate change × contribution margin/outcome` |

```text
Total cost = setup + research + review + implementation + verification
             + recurring operation and maintenance
Economic ROI = (attributable benefit value - total cost) / total cost
Break-even recovered hours = total cost / value per recovered hour
Payback months = upfront cost / (monthly benefit - monthly recurring cost)
```

Simple payback requires sustained positive monthly net benefit. Model delay, adoption ramp, decay, and discounting when material. Cash ROI uses only cash flows. Unknown effects produce a break-even requirement and missing-evidence plan, not an invented benefit.

Include participant pay, supplier fees, accessible setup, models/hosting, authoring/piloting/review, implementation, confirmation, and maintenance. BYO/volunteer work consumes time; invalid studies and unhelpful advice cost effort.

Keep two comparisons separate:

- **Study and fix versus current product:** downstream benefits and full investigation/change costs.
- **Library versus existing research:** differences in cost, latency, quality, and decisions. A fix's entire value cannot be attributed to the library if another method would produce it.

For the second comparison, avoid subtracting replacement costs twice. Replacing a `$1,000` workflow with a `$600` workflow yields `$400` net savings and `67%` return on replacement cost before switching costs. Do not count `$400` as benefit and subtract `$600` again.

## Worked sensitivity example

Assume 120 relevant reports/month, 30 engineering minutes/report, `$100/hour` capacity value. Upfront work: eight research/pilot/review/verification hours, twelve implementation hours, and `$300` participant/provider/model expenses = **$2,300**. Monthly maintenance: two hours plus `$100` service costs = **$300**. Three months after deployment cost **$3,200**.

| Assumed report reduction | Hours recovered/month | Three-month capacity value | Net value | Economic ROI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10% | 6 | $1,800 | −$1,400 | −44% |
| 25% | 15 | $4,500 | $1,300 | 41% |
| 40% | 24 | $7,200 | $4,000 | 125% |

The middle case pays back in **1.9 months after deployment** if sustained. Three-month break-even requires **17.8% fewer reports**, or **10.7 recovered hours/month**. These are capacity economics, not `$4,500` cash savings. The calculations were checked against their inputs, which are the assumptions above. Sensitivity scenarios are neither confidence intervals nor probabilities.

## Primary ROI and DevEx evidence

Historical measurement logic is useful; historical effect sizes are unsuitable calculator defaults.

| Source | Evidence and limit |
| --- | --- |
| [NN/G ROI, 2003](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/return-on-investment-for-usability/) | Separates usability gains and monetary return; values employee time. Collected cases, not a universal multiplier. |
| [NN/G update, 2008](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-roi-declining-but-still-strong/) | Reported gains change with opportunities/design maturity. KPI percentages are not net financial ROI. |
| [NN/G gains, 2020](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-gains-shrinking/) | Variable case-study gains, smaller on average than older collections; no prediction of this product's return. |
| [Mozilla support, 2015](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/testing-decreased-support/) | Firsthand before/after account: roughly 7,000→2,000 monthly **forum questions**, 560 person-hours invested. Multiple research/redesign/content changes; title says “calls,” outcome concerns questions. |
| [UX benchmarking](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/product-ux-benchmarks/) | Baseline tasks, pilot, redesign, remeasure, connect operations. Consistency/uncertainty matter; before/after has possible alternative causes. |
| [MeasuringU planning](https://measuringu.com/services/ux-measurement-stategy-planning/) | Connects KPIs/tasks/benchmarks/ROI; practitioner framework, not expected-return evidence. |
| [DevEx in Action](https://www.michaelagreiler.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/DevEx-in-Action.pdf) | Cross-sectional associations: 219 research-survey completions from 2,213 invitations. Associations are not causal defaults. |

Neither a universal return-per-dollar slogan nor conversion of a usability score establishes intervention value.

## Value through the study lifecycle

Record a **value hypothesis** alongside the human goal: beneficiary, mechanism, comparator, horizon. The framing workflow may inspect permitted issue/support records, propose categories, sample classifications for review, and identify missing denominators/handling time. Unknowns remain explicit; a closed ticket does not prove preventable confusion.

Plan `goal → obstacle → fix → expected operational change → value measure`. Invisible save confirmation, for example, predicts fewer related contacts per 1,000 saves after a fix. Five encounters can suggest that mechanism without establishing causal ticket reduction.

After deployment, record adoption/exposure and compare suitable populations/periods. Prefer randomized rollout where practical; otherwise choose an appropriate comparison/time-series design and disclose seasonality, release changes, and user mix. Audit classifications independently and preserve disagreement. Label results **forecast**, **observed association**, or **supported incremental effect** according to the design.

Reports show human outcomes, normalized operational rates, hours, full costs, uncertainty, and next evidence. ROI cannot convert inconclusive evidence into a pass or erase required access coverage. GEPA receives economic constraints separately; unsupported forecasts are not ground-truth fitness.

The library's validation plan must compare complete study-to-change cycles against existing practice: authoring/review effort, invalid studies, consequential errors, decision latency, implementation cost, and operational outcomes. Report-generation speed matters only with preserved decision quality.
