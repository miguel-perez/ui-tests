# Design artefacts

Approved markup exported from the design artifacts. Each file is the spec for the surface it names; the site's components are built to match it, and `docs/design/site-schema.md` points here.

## Theme rules, in one place

- **The tokens are `tokens.css`.** Light first: ink #111111, one accent #4b3ff0, line #e2e2e2, canvas #f6f6f6, surface white. The roles (moment #b84d00, stop #8b6100, nothing happened #bd1f44, reached #007852, words #7a41af, and the code roles keyword #7444b4, string #27762f, property #00757c) are derived in OKLCH around the accent at one lightness and chroma, each at 4.5:1 or better on the canvas.
- **The site is light only.** `apps/web/app/globals.css` carries the light block alone with `color-scheme: light`, whatever the system setting; nothing on the site reads `prefers-color-scheme` or `data-theme` (issue #43). The Tokyo Night block in `tokens.css` is the artifact viewer's dark alternate under `prefers-color-scheme: dark` and `data-theme="dark"`, not the site's.
- **Colour stays on the data; chrome is neutral.** The accent carries links, buttons, the person marks and off your path. The moment carries the moment to watch, slowed and ranks. Red carries blocked, not reached and a click with no effect. Green-teal carries reached, what worked and the suggested change. Violet carries the person's words. Nothing grey carries meaning; nothing coloured is decoration.
- **The site's own surfaces are light.** The attempt view, the report, the sections, the terminal and code windows are white windows on the canvas with the line colour as their edge. GitHub's chrome is drawn in the site's neutrals.
- **The demo app under test keeps its own cool grey** (`weekboard-light.css`, the `host-*` tokens), so it reads as another product on the light page.
- **The participant chrome is the inverse of the host page, with the author's accent** (`ch-*` tokens): dark on the light Weekboard, with the mint accent. It never shares a panel colour with the host and never carries ui-tests branding. Spec: `docs/discovery/participant-chrome-research.md`.
- **The bar is one small pill.** 40px tall, fully rounded, bottom right by default, draggable, hidden while the person types in the host page; the recording state as its leading segment (dot, time, pause; no microphone during the task), the task summary, Done as a small filled pill and "I couldn't do this" as plain text. The card's last line quotes those two labels.

## The page grid, in one place

Every landing and guide section sits on one container: 1248px wide, centred, with 96px margins at 1440 and 16px at phone width, twelve columns with 24px gutters. The section heading takes the first five columns and its lead the right seven, as the hero does. A figure takes all twelve columns or the right seven; nothing is centred in a narrower column. Pieces inside a section are numbered with a small mono figure in the moment colour before the heading, and each piece's text keeps to 64ch. On a phone everything stacks to one column. A test measures every section's heading, lead and widest figure against this rule.

The vertical rhythm has three steps (issue #51): 24, 48 and 96, which on a phone are 24, 36 and 64. Every section opens with 96 and the last closes with 96 before the footer; a head and each figure are followed by 48, a caption or a note by 24. No rule or motif separates sections; hairlines stay inside figures. The footer is a 5 / 7 row on the same container: the wordmark in the first five, the links in the right seven ending flush with the figures above. Behind everything runs the page's texture, the line colour with a narrow `--wave-accent` tint at each line's centre (`color-mix(in oklab, var(--line) 82%, var(--accent))`, in `tokens.css`).
