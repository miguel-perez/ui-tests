How it works

  1. npx ui-tests run prints the link. It can point at a branch preview, at your laptop through a tunnel, or at your live site.
  2. You send it. Ask a friend to pass it on rather than take it themselves.
  3. Whoever opens it answers the same short screener, so the report still says who they were. Each attempt lands as the person finishes.

Friends are kind and people in tech know too much. Send it beyond your team and beyond tech where you can, and for each problem ask whether your users would hit it too.

How it works

  1. One script tag on your site. It watches for the same trouble the attempt view shows: a dead click, a rage click, a U-turn, a long pause.
  2. Mark the pages it must never interrupt. Checkout, payment and a form in progress are never interrupted anyway.
  3. The second sign of trouble on the same page invites the person. Nothing from before a yes is kept. Their attempt is kept beside the five, not inside.

They show where real users get stuck, not how many do. Treat their problems as leads, and confirm them with a link or a panel run.

How it works

  1. Put a cap in the config. Your deploy and your machine both use your own panel account: add a token once.
  2. npx ui-tests run turns users into each panel's filters and a short screener, prints the plan, and asks before it spends.
  3. People are paid as they finish. Anyone you exclude later stays paid. What is not spent comes back.

Panel people answer screeners for money. Read the Who took part line in the report, and exclude anyone who is plainly not your audience. They stay paid; the count does not.
